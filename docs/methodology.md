# Analytical Methodology & Formulations — Technical Showcase

This document provides transparent definitions and mathematical formulas for all business analytics demonstrated in the **UMKM Retail Analytics Technical Showcase**.

---

## 1. Sales & Revenue Definitions

In retail transaction analysis, precision regarding financial measures is essential:

$$\text{Gross Sales} = \sum (\text{Quantity} \times \text{Unit Price})$$

$$\text{Net Sales} = \sum \Big(\text{Quantity} \times \text{Unit Price} - \text{Discount Amount}\Big)$$

$$\text{Average Transaction Value (ATV)} = \frac{\text{Total Net Sales}}{\text{Unique Transaction Count}}$$

$$\text{Average Items Per Transaction} = \frac{\text{Total Units Sold}}{\text{Unique Transaction Count}}$$

> [!NOTE]
> **Revenue vs. Profit Distinction**: Net sales measures top-line recognized transactional turnover. It does **not** reflect net profit, gross margin, or actual operating cash flow unless Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and payment settlement data are provided.

## 2. Product Performance & Pareto Concentration

To distinguish between high-value drivers and high-velocity fast movers, products are independently ranked across two distinct dimensions:

1. **Rank by Revenue**: Ranked descending by Total Net Sales. Identifies items contributing the largest top-line monetary volume.
2. **Rank by Quantity**: Ranked descending by Total Units Sold. Identifies shelf volume velocity.

$$\text{Revenue Share (\%)} = \frac{\text{Product Net Sales}}{\text{Catalog Total Net Sales}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Cumulative Share (\%)} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \text{Revenue Share}_{i}$$

The cumulative percentage demonstrates the **Pareto Principle** (e.g. what percentage of revenue is generated by the top 20% of SKUs).

## 3. Inventory Movement & Run-Rate Velocity

Inventory analytics evaluates stock exhaustion risk based on historical sales run-rate:

$$\text{Daily Sales Velocity} = \frac{\text{Units Sold in Analysis Period}}{\text{Total Days in Period}}$$

$$\text{Estimated Days of Stock Remaining} = \begin{cases} 
\frac{\text{Stock on Hand}}{\text{Daily Sales Velocity}}, & \text{if } \text{Daily Sales Velocity} > 0 \\
999.0 \text{ (No Movement)}, & \text{if } \text{Daily Sales Velocity} = 0 \text{ and } \text{Stock} > 0 \\
0.0, & \text{if } \text{Stock} \le 0
\end{cases}$$

### Replenishment Alert Rules

- **`OUT_OF_STOCK`**: $\text{Stock on Hand} \le 0$
- **`REORDER_NOW`**: $\text{Stock on Hand} \le \text{Reorder Point}$
- **`LOW_STOCK_WARNING`**: $\text{Reorder Point} < \text{Stock on Hand} \le (\text{Reorder Point} \times 1.5)$
- **`OVERSTOCKED`**: $\text{Estimated Days of Stock} \ge 90$ and $\text{Units Sold} > 0$
- **`ADEQUATE`**: Stock is balanced within operational boundaries.

> [!WARNING]
> **Empirical Run-Rate Disclaimer**: Days of stock remaining is an empirical extrapolation of historical velocity. It is **not** a predictive demand forecast and does not account for seasonality, holidays, or supplier lead-time fluctuations.

## 4. Customer RFM Segmentation

Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis segments identified customer accounts into behavioral cohorts.

### Metric Definitions
- **Recency ($R$)**: Calendar days elapsed between the reference evaluation date and the customer's most recent completed order.
  $$\text{Recency Days} = \text{Date}_{\text{ref}} - \max(\text{Date}_{\text{cust}})$$
- **Frequency ($F$)**: Count of unique `transaction_id` orders placed by the customer within the historical period.
- **Monetary ($M$)**: Total Net Sales spend recorded for the customer.

### Scoring (1 to 5 Quintiles)
Each customer is assigned a score from 1 to 5 using percentile ranks:
- **Recency Score**: Score 5 represents the lowest recency days (most recent); Score 1 represents the highest recency days (longest absent).
- **Frequency Score**: Score 5 represents the highest order count; Score 1 represents the lowest.
- **Monetary Score**: Score 5 represents the highest spend; Score 1 represents the lowest.

### Segment Classification Matrix

| Segment Name | Scoring Criteria | Strategic Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| **Champions** | $R \ge 4$, $F \ge 4$, $M \ge 4$ | High loyalty and high spend. Reward with VIP recognition and early product access. |
| **Loyal Customers** | $F \ge 3$, $M \ge 3$, $R \ge 3$ | Consistent repeat buyers. Cross-sell complementary categories. |
| **Potential Loyalists** | $R \ge 4$, $F \in \{1, 2\}$ | Recent buyers with low frequency. Nurture with targeted re-engagement offers. |
| **At Risk** | $R \le 2$, $(F \ge 3 \text{ or } M \ge 3)$ | High-value past buyers who have lapsed. Send win-back reminders. |
| **Needs Attention** | $R \in \{2, 3\}$, $F \in \{2, 3\}$ | Average recency and frequency. Standard engagement and seasonal promotions. |
| **Lost / Inactive** | $R \le 2$, $F \le 2$ | Infrequent buyers who have not visited in extended periods. Evaluate reactivation ROI. |

### Anonymous Walk-in Treatment
Transactions lacking a valid `customer_id` are audited separately as **Unregistered Walk-in Customers**. They are not merged into a synthetic customer ID to prevent skewing frequency and monetary scores.

## 5. Market Basket Association Analysis

Market basket analysis uncovers item combinations that appear together in transaction baskets.

Given $N$ total transactions:
- **Support of Item A**:
  $$\text{Support}(A) = \frac{\text{Count}(A)}{N}$$
- **Support of Pair $(A, B)$**:
  $$\text{Support}(A \cap B) = \frac{\text{Count}(A \cap B)}{N}$$
- **Confidence $(A \to B)$**:
  $$\text{Confidence}(A \to B) = \frac{\text{Count}(A \cap B)}{\text{Count}(A)}$$
- **Lift Ratio $(A, B)$**:
  $$\text{Lift}(A, B) = \frac{\text{Support}(A \cap B)}{\text{Support}(A) \times \text{Support}(B)}$$

### Interpreting Lift
- $\text{Lift} = 1.0$: Items appear together purely by random chance.
- $\text{Lift} > 1.0$: Items co-occur more frequently than expected by independent probability. Indicates high potential for bundle packaging or shelf adjacency.
- $\text{Lift} < 1.0$: Items are substitutes or rarely purchased together.

> [!NOTE]
> **Correlation vs. Causation**: Co-occurrence proves that items were historically rung up in the same receipt. It does not establish causal motivation or guarantee that a promotion will stimulate incremental sales.
